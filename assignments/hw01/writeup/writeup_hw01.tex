\begin{enumerate}
    \item On the Gisette dataset, train decision trees of maximum depth 1,2,\(\cdots\) up to 12, for a total of 12 
    decision trees.  Use the trained trees to predict the class labels on the training and test sets
    and obtain the training and test misclassification errors.  Plot on the same graph the training and test 
    misclassification errors vs tree depth as two seperate curves.  Report in a table the minimum
    test error and the tree depth for which the minimum was attained.

    We do this in Python, and obtain a plot of the training and test misclassification errors vs tree 
    depth in figure \ref{fig:table1}:

    \begin{figure}[h]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[totalheight=7cm]{../figures/gisette_misclassification_error.png}
        \caption{Misclassification Error vs Tree Depth, Training and Validation: Gisette dataset}
        \label{fig:table1}
    \end{figure}

    The table reporting the minimum test error and the tree depth for which the minium was attained
    are displayed in table \ref{tab:gissette_tree_misclass_error}.

    \begin{table}[h]
        \centering
        \begin{tabular}{ccc}
            \toprule
            Depth & Training Misclassification & Validation Misclassification \\
            \midrule
            10 & 0.014500 & 0.059000 \\
            \bottomrule
        \end{tabular}
        \caption{Lowest misclassification error, Gisette Dataset}
        \label{tab:gissette_tree_misclass_error}
    \end{table}

    In general, the training error decreased as the depth increased.  The validation error more or less followed this
    trajectory, but levelled off for deeper trees.
